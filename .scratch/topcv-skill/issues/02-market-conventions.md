# What CV conventions differ by market and role family?

Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by: —

## Answer

Full findings: [research/market-conventions.md](../research/market-conventions.md) and [research/role-family.md](../research/role-family.md).

**Role family barely matters; market matters a lot.** The assumption that a marketing CV should differ structurally from an engineer's is **not supported**. Law, software engineering, marketing and design all ride one generic skeleton. **Academia is the sole genuine exception** — organized on research/teaching/service pillars with no page cap, a different document in kind. Design is a *weight* exception only: AIGA's own guidance says a designer's résumé should be plainer than average because "the portfolio will always be king".

→ **This collapses the planned template family to one generic template + one academic template**, plus a small set of *conditional sections* (portfolio-link, technical-skills grid, bar admission, publications).

**Market conventions are legally driven and genuinely conflicting.** US: photo harmful (EEOC guidance against employers holding pre-offer photos). UK: omit age, DOB, marital status, nationality — stated outright by the National Careers Service. Germany: photo **legally optional under the AGG but still expected in practice**, with a reported ~19% of recruiters discarding photo-less applications. There is no safe universal default, which vindicates the market-agnostic data-table approach.

**Bonus:** page length flexes by *sector* too (finance/consulting favour 1 page; law/engineering 2) — per Prospects.ac.uk. Still a data-table value, not a template fork.

⚠️ US/UK/EU rows are primary-sourced and strong. The DACH row is SEO-tier; the percentages are indicative, not measured. Austria and Switzerland were not separated from Germany.

## Question

The skill is **market-agnostic by design**: conventions live in a data table it consults, not baked into a template. Build the raw material for that table.

Cover at minimum **Germany/DACH, US, UK, and the wider EU**, and say where each convention is legally driven, culturally driven, or merely habitual:

1. **Photo.** Expected, tolerated, or harmful? Germany is the interesting case — the AGG (Allgemeines Gleichbehandlungsgesetz) pushed against photos, but the Lebenslauf tradition kept them. What is the actual 2026 state, and do German recruiters report a penalty either way? What do US/UK systems do when a photo appears (some ATS reportedly reject or strip them)?
2. **Personal data.** Date of birth, nationality, marital status, address granularity. Legal exposure for the *employer* in each market — that's often what drives the norm.
3. **Length.** Where is one page a hard norm, where is two normal, and does it flex with seniority?
4. **Sections and order.** Which sections are expected, what they're called, and what order they run in. Does a summary/profile paragraph help or waste space?
5. **Role families.** Does the convention genuinely differ between, say, software engineering, marketing, law, academia, and design — or is "different CV per profession" mostly a myth outside academia (where the CV is a different document entirely) and design (where a portfolio carries the weight)? **Push back if the evidence is thin.** A finding of "role barely matters, market matters a lot" would meaningfully simplify the spec.
6. **Language.** When applying in Germany in English vs German — what actually governs the choice?
