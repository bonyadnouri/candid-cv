Legend: **LEGALLY** = required or directly incentivized by statute/regulator
guidance · **CULTURALLY** = no direct legal driver, but a strong, well-evidenced
market norm · **HABIT** = custom repeated by convention with little or no
evidence it's rewarded, penalized, or required · **custom-without-evidence** =
a claim repeated across CV-advice sites with no primary or survey backing found.

## 1. Photo

| Market | Expected / tolerated / harmful | Driver | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **US** | Harmful — a photo is a liability flag for many employers, and its presence signals unfamiliarity with US norms. | **LEGALLY-driven, employer-side.** The EEOC's own guidance states employers should not request applicant photos pre-offer: *"employers should not ask for a photograph of an applicant. If needed for identification purposes, a photograph may be obtained after an offer of employment is made and accepted."* [EEOC — Prohibited Employment Policies/Practices](https://www.eeoc.gov/prohibited-employment-policiespractices). This is about employers *requesting* photos, but it produces a strong downstream norm against *candidates volunteering* one: a photo lets an employer "surmise" race/age/sex pre-interview, which is exactly the fact pattern EEOC guidance treats as discrimination-claim evidence. | Primary (EEOC) for the legal driver. The claim that recruiters affirmatively discard photo resumes is corroborated by a named source (Jon Shields, Jobscan) but is a business practice, not a legal requirement — [The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/resume-picture). |
| **US — ATS handling** | Commonly asserted that ATS "reject" photo resumes; the evidence for a hard technical rejection is weak. What's better supported: ATS parsers frequently mishandle embedded images/complex formatting (garbled or dropped fields), which has the *same effect* as rejection even though it isn't one. | HABIT / **custom-without-evidence** for the strong "ATS auto-rejects photos" claim specifically — no ATS vendor documentation (Workday, Greenhouse, iCIMS, Taleo) was found stating photos trigger rejection. The parsing-garbles-on-complex-formatting claim is better supported. | Secondary, unattributed on the strong claim — e.g. [Jobsolv](https://jobsolv.com/blog/dont-add-photos-on-your-resume-the-ats-will-reject-it), [ATS Fixer](https://atsfixer.com/blog/how-ats-systems-work). Named-source corroboration for the weaker "hard-to-parse" claim: Melody Godfred (career coach) via [The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/resume-picture): *"algorithms absolutely hate any complex formatting."* |
| **UK** | Not expected; commonly advised against; treated as unprofessional outside acting/modelling. No blanket ban, but the norm strongly discourages it. | **LEGALLY-adjacent, CULTURALLY enforced.** No UK statute or ACAS/gov.uk page explicitly bans CV photos (checked [gov.uk Equality Act 2010 guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/equality-act-2010-guidance), [gov.uk discrimination in recruitment](https://www.gov.uk/employer-preventing-discrimination/recruitment), [ACAS recruitment](https://www.acas.org.uk/recruitment) and [ACAS discrimination law in recruitment](https://www.acas.org.uk/recruitment/follow-discrimination-law) directly — none mention photographs). But ACAS does establish the mechanism that produces the norm: equality-monitoring data (which is where protected-characteristic info belongs) *"must [be] kept... separate from the application form and CV."* A photo on the CV itself violates that separation, so the *practice* — not a name-able statute — is what drives photo omission. | Primary for the legal scaffolding (ACAS, gov.uk); no primary source found for the affirmative "recruiters discard photo CVs" claim, which sits at CULTURALLY/**custom-without-evidence** in the recruiter-advice tier — e.g. [PurpleCV](https://purplecv.co.uk/blog/photo-on-a-cv), [UK Careers Fair](https://www.ukcareersfair.com/news/should-i-include-a-recent-photo-with-my-cv). |
| **Wider EU** | Highly country-dependent; not researched to primary-source depth here. France in particular has a live, unsettled debate (a wall of SEO content in French disputes it in both directions with no clear primary source) — this is flagged **not resolved**, do not encode a hard France rule without further digging. | Unclear | No primary source found for France; search returned exclusively CV-advice-blog content on both sides (e.g. riseandhire.com, recrute-talents.fr) — genuinely **custom-without-evidence** territory. Do not ship a confident France rule on this basis. |

### Germany / DACH — the sharpest divergence in the table

⚠️ **Sourcing caveat.** The DACH research agent died before delivering. This section was completed by the main session from German-language sources, but those sources are **SEO-tier career sites, not primary**. The legal position is solid; the behavioural statistics are not independently verified. Sourcing here is materially weaker than the US/UK rows above — do not treat the percentages as reliable.

**The legal position and the practice point in opposite directions, and both are true at once.**

- **Legally: a photo is not required, and employers may not demand one.** Since the AGG (Allgemeines Gleichbehandlungsgesetz) took effect, applicants may not be disadvantaged on the basis of appearance, and employers may not require a Bewerbungsfoto. The AGG protects against discrimination on origin, gender, age, religion, disability and other characteristics. ([business-on.de](https://www.business-on.de/muss-ein-foto-in-den-lebenslauf.html), [jobvector](https://www.jobvector.de/karriere-ratgeber/lebenslauf-ohne-foto/))
- **In practice: the Lebenslauf tradition held.** Reported surveys claim ~70% of German HR staff consider the photo important to first impression, and — the figure that matters most — **an Indeed-attributed statistic that ~19% of recruiters immediately discard applications without a photo.** ⚠️ **Neither figure was traceable to a primary source with disclosed methodology.** Treat as indicative of direction, not as measurement.

**Design implication, and it's a good argument for the market-agnostic approach:** Germany is the case where the US/UK default (never include a photo) is actively harmful, and the US is the case where the German default is actively harmful. There is no safe universal answer — which is precisely why conventions belong in a data table the skill consults rather than in a template. Given the legal-vs-practice tension, the honest behaviour is probably to **surface the trade-off to the user for German applications rather than silently deciding**, since the ~19% figure (if real) cuts against omitting, while the AGG's whole purpose cuts for it.

### DACH — remaining gaps

Not researched to primary-source depth, and flagged as open: German conventions on date of birth, marital status (*Familienstand*), and nationality in the Lebenslauf; the tabular Lebenslauf layout convention; whether a signature and date are still expected; *Zeugnisse* (reference/qualification attachments) as part of the application package; and the German-vs-English language choice when applying to a German employer. Austria and Switzerland were not separated from Germany at all — "DACH" here is really "Germany".

## 2. Personal data (DOB, nationality, marital status, address)

| Market | Norm | Driver | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **US** | Omit DOB, marital status, headshot, SSN, and (increasingly) graduation dates that reveal age. Address is often reduced to city/state or omitted. | **LEGALLY driven.** ADEA (age 40+), Title VII, GINA all create liability if an employer can be shown to have known a protected characteristic pre-decision. EEOC's own general framing: pre-employment inquiries should be *"limited to those essential for determining if a person is qualified for the job,"* and inquiries "regarding race, sex, national origin, age, and religion are irrelevant" pre-hire — [EEOC — Prohibited Employment Policies/Practices](https://www.eeoc.gov/prohibited-employment-policiespractices). | Primary (EEOC). |
| **UK** | Omit age, DOB, marital status, nationality. This is stated as explicit guidance, not just inferred from discrimination law. | **LEGALLY driven**, stated directly by the official careers service: *"You should not include your age, your date of birth, whether you're married or your nationality."* — [National Careers Service — CV sections](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections) (UK government site). Backed by the Equality Act 2010's nine protected characteristics (age, marriage/civil partnership, race, etc.) and the general principle from ACAS that DOB should only be asked on an application form when age is a genuine occupational requirement — [gov.uk — Discrimination in recruitment](https://www.gov.uk/employer-preventing-discrimination/recruitment): *"You can only ask for someone's date of birth on an application form if they must be a certain age to do the job."* | Primary (gov.uk, National Careers Service — both official UK government sources). This is the single best-evidenced finding in the whole US/UK/EU research pass. |
| **Wider EU** | GDPR's data-minimisation principle applies EU-wide to recruitment data generally, arguing the same direction as UK/US (collect/display only what's needed for the decision). This is a real, citable legal basis, not just an inference — but it's a general data-protection principle applied to recruitment *data processing*, not a CV-specific rule, and no country-specific EU source confirming its application to CV content specifically was reached. | **LEGALLY driven** at the principle level. GDPR Article 5(1)(c) requires personal data be *"adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary."* The UK's ICO publishes recruitment-specific guidance applying this ([ICO — Recruitment and selection](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/employment/recruitment-and-selection/), page exists but returned 403 to automated fetch — cite with caution, not independently verified by quote). | GDPR Art. 5(1)(c) is primary EU law; the ICO application of it to recruitment could not be directly quoted (403 error) — treat as "principle confirmed, CV-specific application not independently verified." |

## 3. Length

| Market | Norm | Flexes with seniority? | Driver | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **US** | One page is the strong norm early-career; two pages becomes acceptable/expected with ~10+ years of experience. Academic CVs (a different document entirely, see role-family research) run to 3+ pages with no real cap. | Yes, clearly. | HABIT / CULTURALLY — this is recruiter convention, not law. | Best evidence found is the Purdue OWL CV-vs-resume page, which is about the CV/resume split rather than resume length specifically: *"Entry level resumes are usually limited to a page. CVs, however, often run to three or more pages."* — [Purdue OWL — Writing the CV](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/job_search_writing/resumes_and_vitas/writing_the_cv.html). A dedicated survey source (SHRM or similar) with published methodology on resume-length preference was **not reached** — WebSearch budget was exhausted before this could be run down; treat the "1 page early career" figure as widely-repeated convention, not survey-confirmed. |
| **UK** | Two pages is the commonly cited norm for experienced candidates; one page for graduates/early-career. | Yes, per multiple secondary sources. | HABIT / CULTURALLY. | The official [National Careers Service](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections) page does **not** state a length — this is a genuine gap in official guidance. The "2 pages standard, 1 page for graduates" figure is consistent across several recruiter-advice sites (e.g. credifyuk.com, sausagedog.io) but none carry survey methodology — mark as **custom-without-evidence**, albeit a widely-converged custom. |
| **Wider EU** | The Europass CV format (the EU's own standardized template, promoted via EURES) has no hard page cap but is designed as a fuller document than a US/UK resume; in practice it's most used for EU-institution and cross-border applications rather than typical domestic hiring. | Unclear | Institutional promotion, not evidenced employer preference. | [Europass — for employment and recruitment](https://europass.europa.eu/en/europass-employment-and-recruitment); [EURES — Create your Europass CV](https://eures.europa.eu/jobseekers/create-your-europass-cv_en). Search results asserted "many European employers are familiar with Europass" but produced no evidence of actual employer-side uptake or preference outside EU-institution/EPSO contexts — flag Europass adoption itself as **custom-without-evidence** on the employer side, even though its *existence* and EU promotion are well-documented. |

## 4. Sections and order

| Market | Standard sections / order | Summary/profile paragraph: valued or filler? | Driver | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **US** | "Resume" (not "CV") for standard industry jobs: contact info, (optional summary), experience (reverse-chronological), education, skills. "CV" is reserved almost exclusively for academic job markets and is structured completely differently (education/scholarly identity first, then teaching, publications, grants, conference presentations, service). | Not resolved with survey evidence — the US split on summary-paragraph value is well known in career-advice writing but a methodologically-sound recruiter survey was not reached (WebSearch budget exhausted). Treat as **custom-without-evidence** pending better sourcing. | HABIT/CULTURALLY for section order; the resume/CV *split itself* is closer to institutionally-enforced (academic hiring committees expect the CV format) — see role-family findings once merged. | [Purdue OWL — Writing the CV](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/job_search_writing/resumes_and_vitas/writing_the_cv.html): *"The goal of a resume is to construct a professional identity, the goal of a CV is quite specifically to construct a scholarly identity."* CV sections per the same source: *"your name and contact information, an overview of your education, your academic and related employment..., your research projects (including conference papers and publications), and your departmental and community service."* And: *"The curriculum vitae is used almost exclusively when one is pursuing an academic job."* |
| **UK** | "CV" (this term is used generically, unlike the US where "CV" implies academia). Per official guidance: contact details, introduction, education history, work history, references — order is explicitly flexible by the source itself. | The official source frames it as an "introduction" — *"a few short lines that sum up who you are and what you hope to do"* — rather than a "personal statement," suggesting light value, not filler, not a full profile paragraph either. | HABIT/CULTURALLY for ordering; the section *set* is closer to a soft convention stated by an official body but not framed as mandatory. | [National Careers Service — CV sections](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections) (UK government): *"Your CV should include a section for your contact details, an introduction, your education history, your work history and references... If you have a lot of work experience, you might want to change the order and show off your work history and experience first."* |
| **Wider EU** | The Europass template imposes a fixed section structure (personal info, work experience, education/training, language skills via CEFR levels, digital skills via DigComp, etc.) when used, but adoption outside EU-institution contexts is unconfirmed (see Length section above). | Not established | Institutional (where used) | [Europass — for employment and recruitment](https://europass.europa.eu/en/europass-employment-and-recruitment). |

## 5. Role families — ANSWERED, in [role-family.md](./role-family.md)

**Verdict: role family barely matters. Market matters a great deal.** The "different CV per profession" assumption is **not supported** — law, software engineering, marketing and design all ride the same generic skeleton with content emphasis and a few conditional sections.

- **Academia is the sole genuine structural exception** — organized on research/teaching/service pillars rather than career history, with no page cap. A different document in kind. Well-sourced (five independent university career services).
- **Design is a *weight* exception only.** AIGA's own guidance says a designer's résumé should be *plainer* than average because "the portfolio will always be king" — the opposite of the intuitive assumption.
- **Law** omits summary/skills and adds bar admission and publications — conditional sections, not a fork.
- **Software engineering and marketing** are content variants only, and both rest on SEO-tier sourcing (no professional body found for either).
- **Bonus:** page length flexes by *sector* as well as market and seniority — finance/consulting favour one page, law/engineering two ([Prospects.ac.uk](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/how-to-write-a-cv/)).

**Design implication:** one generic template + one academic template + a conditional-sections list, not a per-profession template family.

## 6. Language (English vs. local language)

| Market | What actually governs the choice | Driver | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wider EU — general rule** | EURES's baseline guidance is to translate the CV into the host country's language. | Institutional/CULTURALLY-recommended, not a legal requirement per se. | [EURES — Create your Europass CV](https://eures.europa.eu/jobseekers/create-your-europass-cv_en): recommends a CV *"translated into the host country language, together with your qualifications."* |
| **Netherlands (contrast case)** | English CVs are widely accepted, especially in tech, multinational, and international-facing roles — a genuine exception to the general-translate rule. | CULTURALLY driven by the country's English proficiency and international business orientation, not a formal carve-out in EURES guidance itself. | Secondary, not primary — [CareerBldr — CV Writing Guide for the Netherlands](https://careerbldr.com/blog/cv-guide-netherlands/). No EURES country-profile page was directly fetched to confirm this officially; treat the general direction as credible (consistent with well-known Dutch English proficiency) but the specific claim as **custom-without-evidence** at the primary-source level. |
| **Germany** | See DACH section once merged — this is one of the six original questions and is being answered there directly (job-ad language / multinational vs. Mittelstand / industry as the actual drivers). | — | — |
| **France (contrast case)** | No single clear official rule found. What surfaced: recruiter-advice sources frame it as company/role-dependent (French for traditional/domestic employers, English acceptable for multinationals and some sectors) rather than a hard requirement either way. One EURES member-state job portal (EURES Italy's TMS platform FAQ) requires English specifically for *its own platform's* applications — that's a platform rule, not a France-wide or general-EU rule, and shouldn't be generalized. | HABIT/CULTURALLY, company-dependent | Secondary only — [CVScore — French or English CV](https://cvscore.fr/en/french-cv/french-or-english); the EURES-platform requirement is primary but narrow in scope — [EURES Italy — CV FAQ](https://eures-tms.politicheattive.lavoro.gov.it/fr/faq-items/comment-dois-je-presenter-mon-cv/): *"Vous devez remplir toutes les sections du CV en anglais"* (applies to that portal, not to applying to French employers generally). |
| **Southern Europe generally** | Not reached — WebSearch budget was exhausted before a language-specific pass beyond France could be run. Do not assume "local language required" without checking further; it's a reasonable prior (consistent with the general EURES translate-to-host-language baseline above) but unconfirmed per-country here. | Unknown | Not researched. |

## Gaps and things NOT to treat as settled

Being explicit about what this pass did *not* establish, so the spec doesn't
silently inherit unverified claims:

- **No SHRM/CIPD/Jobvite-grade recruiter survey was reached** for either US or UK length norms, or for whether a summary/profile paragraph helps or wastes space. Every length figure in this document is convention repeated across recruiter-advice sites, not survey-confirmed. This is flagged per-cell above but bears repeating.
- **ATS "rejects photos"** is weakly evidenced. No ATS vendor (Workday, Greenhouse, iCIMS, Taleo, Lever) technical documentation was found stating this. What's better evidenced is that complex formatting (including embedded images) degrades parsing quality, which produces a similar practical outcome through a different mechanism. The skill should not assert ATS technically "rejects" photos as a hard fact.
- **France's photo norm** is genuinely contested in the sources found, with zero primary sources located on either side (only SEO CV-advice content). Do not encode a France-specific photo rule from this research pass.
- **Europass real-world adoption** by employers (outside EU institutions/EPSO) is asserted by EU-institutional sources but not independently confirmed. Treat Europass as "exists, EU-promoted, adoption outside EU-institution hiring unconfirmed," not as a de facto EU standard employers expect.
- **Wider-EU personal-data norms** (marital status, DOB) were inferred from GDPR's general data-minimisation logic, not confirmed against a specific non-DACH EU country's guidance. This is a plausible but unverified inference.
- This session's **WebSearch budget was exhausted (200/200, shared across the team)** partway through this pass; remaining research after that point relied on WebFetch against known/guessed URLs and DuckDuckGo's non-JS HTML search endpoint as a workaround. Some threads (US/UK survey-grade length data, France, Southern Europe language norms) were cut short by this rather than by an absence of available sources — a follow-up pass with search budget available could likely close these gaps.
